# PFAS Commercial and Competitive Intelligence Extension

## Purpose

This document defines the commercial and competitive intelligence extension of the PFAS Water Technology Landscape.

The objective is to complement the existing scientific and patent analysis with structured evidence about:

- companies;
- commercial technologies;
- partnerships;
- deployments;
- contracts;
- funding;
- acquisitions;
- licensing;
- strategic positioning;
- movement from research into practical application.

This extension is intended to become version 1.1 of the project.

## Intelligence question

Which organizations appear best positioned to commercialize, deploy or enable PFAS water-treatment technologies, and what evidence supports their position?

## Secondary questions

- Which companies are actively offering PFAS treatment products or services?
- Which organizations have moved from research into pilot, field or full-scale deployment?
- Which companies are associated with the most strategically relevant treatment technologies?
- Which organizations combine scientific activity, patents and commercial deployment?
- Which partnerships connect universities, engineering companies, technology developers and public-sector organizations?
- Which organizations appear to be building integrated capture-and-destroy treatment capabilities?
- Which commercial signals indicate increasing commitment to PFAS treatment?
- Which organizations should be monitored for future strategic developments?

## Scope

### Geographic scope

Global, with particular attention to:

- United States;
- Canada;
- Europe;
- commercially relevant activity elsewhere when supported by strong evidence.

### Technology scope

Priority technologies include:

- activated carbon;
- ion exchange;
- electrochemical oxidation;
- supercritical water oxidation;
- plasma;
- photocatalysis;
- sonolysis;
- membranes;
- integrated capture-and-destroy systems.

### Organization types

The analysis may include:

- technology developers;
- water-treatment companies;
- engineering consultancies;
- environmental-services companies;
- equipment manufacturers;
- research organizations;
- universities with strong translational activity;
- public-sector organizations when they influence deployment or technology validation.

## Commercial-intelligence evidence model

For each organization, the following fields should be captured where evidence is available.

### Organization identity

- organization name;
- normalized organization name;
- organization type;
- headquarters country;
- geographic areas of operation.

### Technology position

- PFAS technologies associated with the organization;
- treatment role:
  - capture;
  - concentration;
  - regeneration;
  - destruction;
  - integrated treatment;
- target water or waste matrices;
- position within the treatment value chain.

### Commercial activity

- commercial products;
- commercial services;
- branded technologies;
- treatment systems;
- announced commercial availability;
- deployment locations;
- pilot projects;
- field demonstrations;
- full-scale installations.

### Strategic activity

- partnerships;
- joint ventures;
- research collaborations;
- licensing agreements;
- acquisitions;
- investments;
- grants;
- public-sector contracts;
- customer announcements;
- strategic technology agreements.

### Intellectual and scientific evidence

- relevant patents;
- scientific publications;
- research collaborations;
- links between scientific, patent and commercial activity.

### Evidence metadata

Each individual signal should retain:

- source;
- source URL;
- publication date;
- date accessed;
- evidence type;
- short evidence summary;
- confidence level;
- verification status.

## Confidence levels

### High

Direct evidence from sources such as:

- company announcements;
- government agencies;
- procurement records;
- research institutions;
- patent documents;
- peer-reviewed publications.

### Medium

Credible secondary reporting where the underlying activity is clearly described.

### Low

Unverified claims, promotional statements without supporting detail, or evidence that cannot be independently confirmed.

Low-confidence evidence may be retained for monitoring but should not support major strategic conclusions without corroboration.

Initial candidates identified from the existing scientific and patent landscape include:

- University of Surrey;
- U.S. Environmental Protection Agency.

Additional organizations should be added when strong commercial or technology signals emerge during evidence collection.

## Inclusion criteria

Include an organization when there is credible evidence of at least one of the following:

- active PFAS treatment technology development;
- commercial PFAS treatment products or services;
- pilot, field or full-scale deployment;
- relevant patent activity;
- sustained scientific-to-application trajectory;
- strategically relevant partnership;
- public-sector contract or funded demonstration;
- licensing, acquisition or investment related to PFAS treatment.

## Exclusion criteria

Normally exclude:

- organizations mentioned only incidentally;
- generic environmental consultancies without identifiable PFAS activity;
- isolated academic publications with no sustained technology-development signal;
- unsupported marketing claims;
- organizations whose involvement cannot be verified from credible sources.

## Analytical approach

The first phase should remain evidence-driven and interpretable.

The workflow should:

1. identify candidate organizations;
2. collect structured commercial signals;
3. retain source-level evidence;
4. normalize organization names;
5. map organizations to technologies;
6. classify evidence type and maturity;
7. identify repeated strategic signals;
8. compare organizations across multiple evidence dimensions;
9. produce concise organization intelligence profiles;
10. synthesize cross-company patterns.

No opaque ranking score should be introduced until sufficient evidence exists to justify one.
