# Methodology

## 1. Study design

This project will use an exploratory scientific, technology and competitive intelligence approach to identify and compare emerging technologies for PFAS removal or destruction in water.

The study will combine evidence from:

- scientific publications;
- patent documents;
- corporate websites;
- research project databases;
- public funding announcements;
- business and technology news.

The analysis will focus on patterns of activity, organisational positioning, technology maturity and links between research, intellectual property and commercial development.

## 2. Analysis period

The primary analysis period is 2018–2026.

Earlier records may be included when they provide necessary historical context for:

- foundational technologies;
- important patent families;
- long-running research programmes;
- established organisations entering the PFAS treatment field.

## 3. Geographic coverage

The study will have global coverage, with particular attention to:

- Europe;
- United States;
- Canada.

Geographic analysis may consider:

- author affiliations;
- applicant and assignee locations;
- headquarters of companies;
- project locations;
- pilot and commercial deployment sites.

## 4. Technology taxonomy

Records will be classified using a provisional technology taxonomy.

### Adsorption

- Activated carbon
- Ion-exchange resins
- Biochar
- Advanced porous materials

### Membranes

- Reverse osmosis
- Nanofiltration
- Functionalised membranes

### Destructive technologies

- Electrochemical oxidation
- Plasma treatment
- Photocatalysis
- Sonolysis
- Supercritical water oxidation
- Thermal or catalytic processes

### Combined processes

- Capture followed by destruction
- Membrane concentration followed by treatment
- Other integrated treatment trains

The taxonomy may be revised as new terminology, subcategories and hybrid processes are identified.

## 5. Data collection

Search strategies will be documented separately for each source type.

Initial tools and sources may include:

- OpenAlex for scientific literature metadata;
- Espacenet and WIPO PATENTSCOPE for patent searching;
- corporate and university websites;
- public research project databases;
- public news sources;
- Zotero for reference management.

Search dates, query strings, filters and export methods will be recorded to support reproducibility.

## 6. Inclusion criteria

A record may be included when it:

- concerns PFAS in water or a directly relevant aqueous stream;
- describes removal, separation, concentration, degradation, destruction or mineralisation;
- falls within the defined time period, unless historical context is necessary;
- provides sufficient metadata for classification and analysis;
- contributes evidence about scientific, technological or commercial activity.

## 7. Exclusion criteria

A record may be excluded when it:

- concerns PFAS detection only, without a treatment component;
- focuses exclusively on non-aqueous applications;
- is unrelated to PFAS treatment;
- lacks enough information for reliable classification;
- duplicates another record;
- cannot be used under the applicable source terms or licence.

## 8. Data processing

Raw records will be preserved locally when permitted.

Processed datasets may include fields such as:

- title;
- year;
- source type;
- authors or inventors;
- organisations;
- country;
- technology category;
- treatment objective;
- development stage;
- identifier or public URL;
- notes on relevance.

Processing steps may include:

- duplicate removal;
- organisation name normalisation;
- country standardisation;
- taxonomy assignment;
- missing-value review;
- separation of capture and destruction technologies;
- identification of patent families where feasible.

## 9. Analysis

The initial analysis may include:

- annual publication trends;
- annual patent activity;
- activity by technology category;
- activity by organisation;
- geographic distribution;
- collaboration patterns;
- comparison of scientific, patent and commercial signals;
- identification of organisations active across multiple evidence types.

The project will prioritise interpretable analysis before introducing advanced machine learning or language-model workflows.

## 10. Technology maturity assessment

Technology maturity will be assessed cautiously using public evidence such as:

- laboratory studies;
- prototype development;
- pilot projects;
- demonstration projects;
- commercial products or services;
- announced deployments;
- partnerships, licensing or investment activity.

Any maturity classification will be treated as an analytical judgement rather than a verified engineering assessment.

## 11. Reproducibility

The repository will document:

- research questions;
- methodology;
- search strategies;
- data fields;
- processing decisions;
- Python dependencies;
- notebooks and scripts;
- known limitations.

Generated analyses should be reproducible from publishable or openly redistributable data wherever possible.

## 12. Copyright, licensing and confidentiality

The public GitHub repository may include:

- original methodology and commentary;
- search strategies;
- Python code;
- charts created from redistributable data;
- processed metadata permitted by the source licence;
- links and identifiers for public records;
- original summaries and conclusions.

The repository should not include:

- full-text journal articles;
- copyrighted figures or tables copied from publications;
- bulk exports from subscription databases;
- proprietary abstracts or enhanced metadata when redistribution is restricted;
- confidential employer information;
- internal reports;
- credentials, API keys or personal data;
- documents downloaded under licences that prohibit redistribution.

Where source data cannot be redistributed, the repository will document how an authorised user can reproduce the search independently.

## 13. Limitations

This study will not provide:

- exhaustive coverage of all relevant records;
- legal conclusions;
- freedom-to-operate analysis;
- patent validity analysis;
- regulatory approval assessment;
- detailed process engineering;
- definitive techno-economic comparison.

Results will reflect the coverage and quality of the selected public sources, search strategies and classification decisions.
