# Research Question

## Primary question

Which emerging technologies for the removal or destruction of PFAS in water show the strongest scientific, technological and business activity, and which organisations appear best positioned?

## Secondary questions

1. Which PFAS treatment technologies have shown the greatest growth in scientific publication activity since 2018?

2. Which technologies show the greatest patenting activity?

3. Which companies, universities and research organisations are the most active in each technology area?

4. Which organisations appear to connect scientific research, patent activity and commercial development?

5. Which technologies are primarily focused on PFAS capture, and which aim to destroy or mineralise PFAS?

6. Which technologies are being combined into integrated treatment processes?

7. Which technologies appear to be moving from laboratory research towards pilot-scale or commercial deployment?

8. What emerging organisations, research collaborations and technology developers should an R&D or innovation team monitor?

## Technology scope

The initial technology taxonomy includes:

### Adsorption

- Activated carbon
- Ion-exchange resins
- Biochar
- Advanced porous materials

### Membranes

- Reverse osmosis
- Nanofiltration
- Functionalised membranes

### Destructive technologies

- Electrochemical oxidation
- Plasma treatment
- Photocatalysis
- Sonolysis
- Supercritical water oxidation
- Thermal or catalytic processes

### Combined processes

- Capture followed by destruction
- Membrane concentration followed by treatment
- Other integrated treatment trains

## Geographic scope

The study will have global coverage, with particular attention to:

- Europe
- United States
- Canada

## Time period

The primary analysis period is 2018–2026.

Earlier records may be included when necessary to explain the development of a technology, patent family or organisation.

## Intended audience

The simulated client is an R&D director, technology manager or innovation leader evaluating:

- emerging treatment technologies;
- possible research collaborations;
- technology licensing opportunities;
- investment targets;
- suppliers or technology partners;
- areas requiring continued monitoring.

## Study limitations

This project is an exploratory and reproducible intelligence study.

It is not intended to be:

- an exhaustive systematic review;
- a regulatory assessment;
- an engineering design study;
- a detailed techno-economic assessment;
- a legal opinion;
- a patent validity analysis;
- a freedom-to-operate study.

The conclusions will depend on the coverage, quality and availability of the public data sources used.
